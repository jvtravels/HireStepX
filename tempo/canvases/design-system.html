01

The discipline

One rule, applied everywhere. If you remember nothing else, remember this.

Indigo is interactive.
Copper is editorial.
Never mix.
  • IndigoCTAs · links · focus rings · brand identifiers (logo wordmark, score numbers as state) · selection
  • CopperThe italic accent word in a hero. A "highlighted number or fact" — once per moment. Never twice on the same screen.
  • CoalPrimary text. Warm dark, never pure black.
  • Indigo-graySecondary text. Tints body copy with brand identity, subliminally.
  • DefaultsErrors are red. Success is green. Don't burn brand budget on status.

Contrast & accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA requires 4.5:1 for body text. AAA prefers 7:1. Every approved pairing checks out.

15.9:1 · AAA

Coal on Cream

Hero text, primary copy. The default pair.

--coal / --cream

7.4:1 · AAA

Indigo-gray on Cream

Body copy, secondary text. Brand-tinted reading.

--indigo-gray / --cream

9.1:1 · AAA

Indigo on Cream

Links, interactive text. Always meets AAA.

--indigo / --cream

5.4:1 · AA

Copper on Cream

Italic accent words, headlines only. AA pass.

--copper / --cream

12.5:1 · AAA

White on Indigo

Primary CTA text. Universal pass.

--white / --indigo

17.4:1 · AAA

Coal on White

Card surfaces. Maximum readability.

--coal / --white

Do & don't

The discipline rule applied to common situations. Save the brand from drift.

Do

One copper moment per screen.

The italic accent word is the signature. Don't dilute it.

Hero: "Reset your password"
Score numeral: 62
Pick one. Not both.
Don't

Stack copper on warnings, icons, AND text.

When everything is highlighted, nothing is.

❌ Copper italic accent + copper warning icon + copper time-clock + copper avatar + copper "replace" link — all on one screen.
Do

Make every clickable thing indigo.

Predictability is premium. Buttons, links, focus rings, score numbers (interactive on click).

View report · → Continue
Don't

Use copper on click targets.

Copper is editorial — a label for a fact, not an invitation to act.

❌ "Replace" link in copper · ❌ "View all" CTA in copper · ❌ Tab navigation in copper.
Do

Tint secondary text with indigo-gray.

Body copy stays subliminally on-brand. Mercury and Substack do exactly this.

"You have a strong foundation. With a few improvements, you'll stand out."
Don't

Use a neutral gray for secondary text.

Generic grays make every SaaS look the same. The indigo-tint is an inch of identity per paragraph.

❌ "Generic Tailwind gray-500." Looks like every other dashboard.

11

Why these colors

The cultural and competitive logic behind every choice.

Indigo is historically Indian.

The dye is literally named after the country — Sanskrit nīla → Greek indikón. India's signature export for 2,000+ years.

Using it isn't cliché in the way saffron is. It's quietly, culturally rooted.

No competitor in our space owns indigo — Yoodli is blue, Final Round is blue/black, Pramp is orange, Big Interview is red. We can be the first.

Copper is the perfect counterweight.

Warm where indigo is cool. Earthen where indigo is sky. Indian-aware (terracotta, copper, brass are visual signatures of Indian craft). And already at home in our cream-bg world.

The two colors fight beautifully — like a Banarasi or Pochampally weave. Subconsciously Indian without ever being literal.

Indigo says depth, calm, authority, premium. Copper says heritage, warmth, fact. Together they say: this product was made by someone who knows.

# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Indian job-seekers preparing for interviews: engineering freshers and campus
placements, IT-services lateral hires (TCS/Infosys/Wipro-style), product/GCC
aspirants, MBA/bank-PO/BPO candidates, and English-as-second-language
speakers building interview confidence. They land on marketing/SEO pages
(like /questions) from a Google search for a specific company or question
type, often on mobile, often anxious about an upcoming interview, scanning
for "does this have what I need" before committing time. Purchase intent is
low-friction (₹9/session) but attention is scarce — these pages compete with
Glassdoor, Naukri, GeeksforGeeks, and coaching-site aggregators for the same
query, so clarity and scannability decide whether the visitor stays.

## Product Purpose

HireStepX is an AI-powered mock interview platform. The marketing/SEO
surface (company × role interview-question pages, city guides, blog,
salary guides) exists to rank for high-intent search queries and convert
readers into a free trial session. Success on a page like /questions is:
the visitor immediately understands what's here, finds their target
company/question type fast, and either reads real question content or
clicks into a mock-interview session — not bounces because the page reads
as dense or hard to parse.

## Brand Personality

Warm, direct, credible. Approachable without being saccharine; talks to an
anxious job-seeker like a knowledgeable friend, not a corporate brochure or
a hard-sell coaching institute. Confidence comes from real, specific content
(actual questions, real STAR breakdowns) rather than marketing language.

## Anti-references

- Generic ed-tech / Indian coaching-site clutter: dense, ad-cluttered,
  low-trust layouts (Testbook-style, GeeksforGeeks-style walls of text with
  no hierarchy) that make content feel like a chore to extract.
- Generic AI-SaaS template: the cream/serif/terracotta or
  dark-with-neon-accent look shared by countless interchangeable AI
  startups — HireStepX should not read as a template reskin.

## Design Principles

- Scannability over density: a visitor deciding in 3 seconds whether this
  page has their answer should be able to tell instantly — hierarchy and
  whitespace do the sorting, not the reader.
- Show, don't tell: real question content and real company specifics build
  more trust than adjectives ("comprehensive", "expert-crafted").
- One primary path per page: every page should make it obvious what to read
  next and what to click next, not present many competing options at once.
- Practice what you preach: as an interview-prep product, the writing and
  layout should itself model clarity and structure.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No explicit WCAG target stated by the user; treat WCAG 2.1 AA as the floor
(matches CLAUDE.md's completed accessibility audit for the app). Many users
are on mid/low-end Android over slower connections — keep pages light and
legible without relying on JS for core content visibility.
